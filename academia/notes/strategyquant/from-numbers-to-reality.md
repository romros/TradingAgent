# From numbers to reality

A variable can explain a market and still be unusable as a trading signal. The agent must ask when the value became knowable, not merely which economic month it describes.

For every external driver:

1. Record observation time, publication time, revisions and reporting coverage.
2. Shift the signal to the earliest conservative execution time.
3. Preserve historical vintages when revisions are material.
4. Separate three roles: entry trigger, risk/regime filter, and post-trade explanation.
5. Reject any backtest that aligns delayed data to its reference month as though it were already public.

Applied examples:

- Weekly broad-dollar data require a publication buffer; ignoring it created lookahead in the first XAU formulation.
- ECB–Fed policy rates are timely enough to test, but their frozen EUR/USD rule lost gross and was rejected before SQ.
- Official gold purchases normally arrive about two months late. They may describe a slow XAU regime from publication onward, but cannot be a contemporaneous weekly trigger.

The practical standard is: causality makes a hypothesis worth testing; only point-in-time availability, net expectancy, temporal stability and executable sizing make it a strategy.
